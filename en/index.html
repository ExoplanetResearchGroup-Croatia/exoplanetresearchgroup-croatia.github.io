About the group and its members

ERG Croatia brings together amateur astronomers from across Croatia with the aim of better understanding extrasolar planets and contributing to the databases that describe them. We monitor already confirmed planets, refine their parameters and take part in confirming new ones. The group is based in Rijeka, and astronomical societies from Pula and Čakovec also take part in its work.

History

The group was founded in early 2026 as ETD Rijeka and initially worked in the Rijeka area. At the XXXIV Alpe Adria Meeting on 27 September 2026 it expanded to Pula, when members of the Astronomical Society Istra Pula joined. On 21 November 2026 the group was renamed ERG Croatia, and astronomers from the Astronomical Society Vega in Čakovec joined as well.

Aim

The group aims to keep adding new measurements to the Exoplanet Transit Database (ETD) and to contribute to the confirmation of new extrasolar planets. Equally important to us is educating pupils and anyone interested, through workshops in schools and towns.

What we do

  • Transit observations

    We record the field around a star whose planet passes in front of it, covering the whole transit plus 30 to 60 minutes before it begins and after it ends. The method is described in more detail in the Observing guide.

  • Analysis and modelling

    We process the images with differential photometry in AstroImageJ and submit the results to the ETD, ExoClock and AAVSO databases.

  • Education and outreach

    We give talks and workshops and work with interested pupils across Croatia. The aim is to promote science and to share the knowledge and experience we have gained.

How to get involved

  1. Who can join

    Anyone interested who is willing to contribute to the group's work can join, and in return the group supports its members.

  2. What you need

    Interest and willingness to work matter most. Basic knowledge of astronomy and the night sky and experience with a telescope are useful. In terms of equipment, a motorised telescope with astrophotography gear, or a more powerful computer for data processing, is helpful.

  3. First step

    Contact us by e-mail or through the group's social media, or get in touch with one of the member astronomical societies. You will then be introduced to the group and its work.

Services and useful links

  • Exoplanet Transit Database (opens in a new tab)

    A database of transit light curves with predictions of upcoming transits, run by the Variable Star and Exoplanet Section of the Czech Astronomical Society.

  • ExoClock (opens in a new tab)

    A project in which professional and amateur observers keep the transit times of the planets the Ariel space mission will observe up to date.

  • NASA Exoplanet Archive (opens in a new tab)

    A catalogue of confirmed exoplanets with their parameters and data from missions such as Kepler and TESS.

Our tools

  • TransitPlanner

    A tool for planning transit observations for your location and equipment and for creating sequences for N.I.N.A. and KStars (Ekos).
