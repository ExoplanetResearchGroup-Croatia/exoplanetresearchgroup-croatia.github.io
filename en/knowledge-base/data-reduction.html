Knowledge base / Data reduction

Data reduction

Draft, under review. This text was written from general knowledge and has not yet been reviewed by the group.

Calibrating the frames

Bias and dark frames are subtracted from every image, which is then divided by a flat. This removes electronic noise, the camera's thermal signal, differences in pixel sensitivity and dust shadows on the optics.

Photometry

Differential photometry measures the brightness of the target relative to comparison stars on the same frame, so changes in atmospheric transparency largely cancel out.

  • A circular aperture is placed around each star, and the sky background is measured in a ring around it.
  • Comparison stars should be similar in colour and brightness to the target and must not be variable.
  • The result is the relative brightness of the target over time: the light curve.

Model fitting

A transit model is fitted to the light curve. It gives the mid-transit time, depth, duration and their uncertainties. A slow trend caused by the target's changing altitude (airmass) is often removed at the same time.

For publication, times are usually converted to Barycentric Julian Date (BJDTDB) so that they can be compared with other observations.

Software

Commonly used free programs include AstroImageJ (opens in a new tab), HOPS (opens in a new tab) and C-Munipack (opens in a new tab).

Submitting results

The Exoplanet Transit Database (opens in a new tab) accepts light curves and fits a transit model to them itself. ExoClock (opens in a new tab) accepts results for planets on the Ariel mission target list. Check each project's instructions before submitting.
